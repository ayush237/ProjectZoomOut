| 2026-09-02 | **Image selection is not delegable to a machine. No brightness detector — the answer to WP20.1's ruling request is the one already on the books** | Answers Pipeline Manager's measured finding that **8 of 18 first candidates (~44%) breach the no-glow rule**, Leaf 11 being the exact drift WP20 sampled as a risk. **The 2026-08-29 ruling did not fail here; its precondition was removed.** That ruling kept no-glow as prompt-and-review on the explicit reasoning that *"three candidates per Leaf means drift costs a click"* — **a justification conditional on a human doing the clicking.** WP20.1 delegated the pick and nothing clicked. So this is not a newly-discovered gap, it is an old mitigation quietly separated from the condition that made it sound, and the fix is to restore the condition rather than to replace it. **Three reasons the detector is the wrong instrument.** *It inverts the stakes ordering* — identifiable people is the legal condition and is deliberately guarded at the **input** because a detector flags the wrong thing; building mechanical enforcement for a **style** rule while the legal one rests on prompt discipline spends the expensive tool on the cheapest problem. *The proxy misfires both ways* — a daylit window, a bright sky, a white wall and a screen that is merely on are all large uniform bright regions and none are glow, while Leaf 14 is "screen bloom only", subtle enough that any threshold catching it catches every legitimately bright image. **This is face detection and OCR in a third costume, rejected on the same grounds both previous times.** *And 44% of **first** candidates is a generation signal, not a selection one* — WP20 already found the sixth anchor "teaches a tendency, not a rule", so the cheap lever is upstream in the anchor set and the prompt, not a filter at the end that costs a fetch per image and can silently reject good candidates. **Nothing is blocked:** the founder delegated this Track explicitly, every Leaf still carries two alternatives, and eight overrides is eight clicks | A brightness/uniform-region detector (a proxy that fails in both directions, on the least consequential of the four conditions); leaving the delegated pattern in place for later books (its own measurement says 44% breach); regenerating Track 42's images for a seventh anchor (pays twice — the text is what would drive a regeneration, and it is not being regenerated) |
| 2026-09-02 | **Where a check covers one of several stated conditions, its name says which one — this is the fourth occurrence and it is now a rule, not an observation** | WP20.1's selector reports `reason: "first candidate passing guardrails"` in the founder's review list, while exactly one of the four stated image conditions is mechanically checked. **Pipeline Manager is not the problem here and that is the point** — `selection.py`'s own docstring says plainly that *"the guardrails cannot catch that particular drift"*, and the completion report measured the gap at 44% rather than letting the phrase stand. **The honesty existed; it just did not reach the surface the founder reads.** The prior three: WP20's criterion *"no amber, no text, no identifiable people — checked"* (ruled 2026-08-29 as overclaiming three checks where one exists), `repository.py`'s comment claiming `purge_raw_text` was wired to a terminal node, and `machinesUpdateDraftsOnly`'s comment (fixed in WP15.5). **Four occurrences across four packages by three different sessions is not a vigilance problem, it is a naming default** — a plural noun for a singular guarantee reads as complete to everyone except the person who wrote it. So: `"first candidate, amber-checked"`, and the same test applied to any future name covering a subset. **The cost of getting this wrong is specific:** a reviewer who believes four conditions are enforced reviews less carefully than one who knows it is one | Fixing the string alone (three prior instances say the shape recurs); a naming-convention document (nobody reads one — the rule belongs beside the reviewing habit that catches it) |

| 2026-08-28 | **Correctness and completeness stay separate rules — empty scenario options are `checkAllSlidesPopulated`'s business, not `checkExactlyOneCorrectOption`'s** | Confirms Manager's scope call on WP15.5, and corrects an ambiguity in my own criterion: *"must go red for this rule"* could be read as requiring `checkExactlyOneCorrectOption` to stay publish-aware, which was not what I meant. **Empty options are absent, not wrong** — a rule about whether a populated scenario is correct should not also police whether one exists, and the file already drew that line. | 2026-08-29 | **The image guardrails move to the prompt, not the pixels — and the acceptance criterion was overclaiming** | Ruled on WP20's honest report that only `check_reward_amber` is mechanical while text, faces and glow rest on the prompt, the anchors and a human eye. **My criterion said "no amber, no text, no identifiable people — checked, and say how", which implied three mechanical checks when only one exists.** Saying so rather than letting "checked" stand was the criterion working. **The ruling separates them by stakes.** *Identifiable people* is the legal one and **cannot usefully be checked in the output** — a stylised figure with a face is not a real person, so a face detector flags the wrong thing, and recognising real people would need a database of them. **Guard the input instead: assert the generated image prompt contains no author name and no real-person reference.** That is deterministic, cheap, and it is the only route by which a real likeness could enter, so it is a stronger guarantee than inspecting pixels. *Text in images* and *the no-glow rule* stay prompt-and-review: three candidates per Leaf means drift costs a click, and OCR was correctly rejected. **What changes is the honesty of the record** — those two are constrained and reviewed, not checked, and future criteria must not say "checked" when they mean that | Face detection (flags stylised faces, misses the actual requirement); OCR (rejected in WP19 on sound grounds); declaring the criterion met (three conditions, one check) |

| 2026-08-29 | **A fix belongs everywhere its concern has a sibling — four of WP20's seven defects were one-sided fixes** | The SDK retry fix landed in the text client and not the image client. The find-then-skip idempotency guard was in `write_drafts_to_cms` and not the asset path, where it failed at Leaf 11 of 18 — **the identical failure that node's own comment documents.** The answer-length check ran in the retrofit command and not the graph. **The codebase knew about every one of these; it knew in the wrong file.** This is a distinct failure from this project's usual one — that is a guarantee enforced at one *layer* and assumed at the next, this is a guarantee enforced in one of two *parallel* implementations of the same concern. **The rule: when a fix or guard goes into a client, a path or a node, find its sibling before closing.** Where a pair exists, tests cover both | Case-by-case vigilance (four occurrences in one package is not a vigilance problem) |
