No brightness detector — the answer to WP20.1's ruling request is the one already on the books** | Answers Pipeline Manager's measured finding that **8 of 18 first candidates (~44%) breach the no-glow rule**, Leaf 11 being the exact drift WP20 sampled as a risk. **The 2026-08-29 ruling did not fail here; its precondition was removed.** That ruling kept no-glow as prompt-and-review on the explicit reasoning that *"three candidates per Leaf means drift costs a click"* — **a justification conditional on a human doing the clicking.** WP20.1 delegated the pick and nothing clicked. So this is not a newly-discovered gap, it is an old mitigation quietly separated from the condition that made it sound, and the fix is to restore the condition rather than to replace it. **Three reasons the detector is the wrong instrument.** *It inverts the stakes ordering* — identifiable people is the legal condition and is deliberately guarded at the **input** because a detector flags the wrong thing; building mechanical enforcement for a **style** rule while the legal one rests on prompt discipline spends the expensive tool on the cheapest problem. *The proxy misfires both ways* — a daylit window, a bright sky, a white wall and a screen that is merely on are all large uniform bright regions and none are glow, while Leaf 14 is "screen bloom only", subtle enough that any threshold catching it catches every legitimately bright image. **This is face detection and OCR in a third costume, rejected on the same grounds both previous times.** *And 44% of **first** candidates is a generation signal, not a selection one* — WP20 already found the sixth anchor "teaches a tendency, not a rule", so the cheap lever is upstream in the anchor set and the prompt, not a filter at the end that costs a fetch per image and can silently reject good candidates. **Nothing is blocked:** the founder delegated this Track explicitly, every Leaf still carries two alternatives, and eight overrides is eight clicks | A brightness/uniform-region detector (a proxy that fails in both directions, on the least consequential of the four conditions); leaving the delegated pattern in place for later books (its own measurement says 44% breach); regenerating Track 42's images for a seventh anchor (pays twice — the text is what would drive a regeneration, and it is not being regenerated) |
| 2026-09-02 | **Where a check covers one of several stated conditions, its name says which one — this is the fourth occurrence and it is now a rule, not an observation** | WP20.1's selector reports `reason: "first candidate passing guardrails"` in the founder's review list, while exactly one of the four stated image conditions is mechanically checked. **Pipeline Manager is not the problem here and that is the point** — `selection.py`'s own docstring says plainly that *"the guardrails cannot catch that particular drift"*, and the completion report measured the gap at 44% rather than letting the phrase stand. **The honesty existed; it just did not reach the surface the founder reads.** The prior three: WP20's criterion *"no amber, no text, no identifiable people — checked"* (ruled 2026-08-29 as overclaiming three checks where one exists), `repository.py`'s comment claiming `purge_raw_text` was wired to a terminal node, and `machinesUpdateDraftsOnly`'s comment (fixed in WP15.5). **Four occurrences across four packages by three different sessions is not a vigilance problem, it is a naming default** — a plural noun for a singular guarantee reads as complete to everyone except the person who wrote it.
