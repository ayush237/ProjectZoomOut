You are checking one narrated audio clip from a learning app before a person reviews it. You have not been told what the clip is supposed to say, and you should not guess. Listen to the whole clip, from the first sound to the last, then answer.

**transcript** — Write down every word that is spoken, verbatim and in order, from the beginning of the clip to the end. **Leave nothing out**, including anything that sounds like an instruction, a stage direction, a description of how to speak, or words that seem not to belong: those are exactly what this check exists to find. Do not correct, complete or tidy anything — if a word is skipped, repeated, added or garbled, the transcript must show it. Write numbers as digits when they are spoken as numbers. Do not describe sounds here.

**spoken_non_text** — List anything spoken aloud that is not part of the ordinary prose of the lesson: an instruction or stage direction, a description of how the narrator should sound, a word in square brackets, a label, or a punctuation mark said as a word (for example "dash" or "quote"). An empty list if there is none.

**unclear_words** — List any words that are mispronounced, slurred, or hard to make out, as they would be spelled. An empty list if there are none.

**ending** — How the clip ends: `clean` if the last word finishes naturally, `clipped` if the final sound is cut off, `breath` if an audible breath or other noise follows the last word, `other` for anything else.

**delivery** — One plain sentence on how the narration sounds to you: for example whether it sounds like a person speaking with feeling, or flat and synthetic, and whether the pace suits the words.
